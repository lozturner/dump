EX Experience start here ▼
What am I actually experiencing right now?
State the raw situation — not your interpretation of it, just what's happening. What do you observe? What are the facts on the ground? Don't judge yet. Don't solve yet. Just: what is the thing in front of me?
I haven't seen my son in a significant stretch. Contact is being blocked. The court case is live and moving. I'm building a documentation system on a phone, one-handed, between court dates. Multiple threads are running in parallel — court, complaints, kids' day-to-day, money — and they don't wait for me.
C Concepts what applies ▼
What principles, patterns, or knowledge apply here?
Match the experience to structure you already have. What mental models fit? What have you learned before that's relevant? What domain rules govern this situation? You're not solving yet — you're locating the experience inside what you already know.
Courts reward documented patterns, not emotional accounts. Enforcement requires Form C79 and a threshold of denial events on record. Executive function gaps mean I need systems that run without me, not willpower. The sysadmin lesson: logs beat memory, always. The hub exists specifically to hold these logs so they're there when I need them.
PF Problem Frame the hinge ▼
What is the REAL problem — not the surface one?
Strip the noise. The surface problem is almost never the actual problem. Ask: what gap, if closed, would make this no longer a problem? Restate it in one clean sentence. This is the most important step. Get it wrong and all effort after it goes sideways. Right-framed problems often answer themselves.
Surface: "Why won't they let me see him?"

Actual: "I need a documented, dated pattern of denial in a form a court will act on."

The path page is built from problem frames. Each node on it IS a PF → AL pair. The whole hub is a machine for converting messy experience into clean frames.
EG Examples test the frame ▼
What does this look like concretely? Can you give one example?
Generate specific, concrete examples. What would a solution look like in practice? What have others done? Simulate it. If you can't produce a concrete example, the problem frame is wrong — go back to PF. Examples are reality-checks on your framing. They also generate new experience, which sends you around the loop again.
The picture page: 47 days logged, multiple missed marked with reasons. The court bundle: incidents with dates, times, what was said. C79 applications others have filed. The "enforcement order" node on the path page with its specific actions. Each of these is a concrete example — testable, referrable, real.
AL Action / Learning the output ▼
What's the one thing I now do, or the one thing I now know?
Not a list — the one thing. Name the action you take, or the understanding you've gained. This is the output of the loop. After AL, you either act and that act becomes new Experience (EX again), or you pause with new knowledge and loop back deliberately. AL is not the end. It's what makes the next loop sharper.
Action: Document the next denial within 24 hours. Timestamp. Who said what. Any witnesses. Into the picture page.

Learning: Building the hub is not a distraction from the real thing — it is the documentation infrastructure. Every hour on it is an hour building the evidential record. That's not escape. That's strategy.

Low resolution · life direction
EX I'm cut off from my son.  C Courts, patience, documentation.  PF I need enforcement evidence, not just feelings.  EG The path page.  AL Complete the next node.
Mid resolution · today's task
EX Contact was denied again this weekend.  C I need a formal log entry.  PF What the log needs: date, time, reason given, any witnesses.  EG The last entry I made that was clear.  AL Write it now, before the details go soft.
High resolution · this exact moment
EX I'm writing a sentence to the court.  C Formal, factual, dated, no emotion.  PF This sentence needs to achieve X specifically.  EG The last letter that got a response.  AL Revise or send.
