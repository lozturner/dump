Dad CVs.
CVs for the people who can't read them.

Separated out from the gym-stand thread · 4 surfaces · 1 personal seed
Premise. CVs are dead in the age of AI. Nobody needs to write one for a job any more. So flip the artefact: write it for your children instead. What you provide, what you're good at, what you'd want them to know if they ever went looking. One community site for any caregiver without contact. One personal sister site for the founder — seeded into his own timeline so his kids can find it when they search. Philanthropy = using what you have to build a system other people can survive inside. Reese Witherspoon did it with publishing. Do it with fatherhood.
🔴 Core Concept

A CV isn't for an employer. It's for whoever inherits you. The format already exists — experience, skills, references, what you provide. Reframe it as a document a child can read later, whether you're estranged, dead, or just never got the words out.

↳ One community platform. Anyone without contact with their children can submit a CV / letter. Caregivers too, not only dads.
↳ AI-assisted — you tell it what you've done, it structures it. Same trick as the gym-stand stack generator, different product.
↳ Output = a page a child could Google their own name and find. A record that says "someone was thinking about you."
🔵 Four Surfaces

Not one site — a ring of them. Each does a different job. Together they cover charity, data, personal, and press.

01 · dadcvs.*
Community Charity

Anyone submits a CV for a child they can't reach. Caregivers, dads, mums. Free. The cornerstone — no profit, drives the press.

02 · compare.dadcvs.*
Data mirror Revenue

Normalises the CV data. "Here's what other dads in your income / age / postcode bracket say they provide." Either bait (social-intrusive) or mirror (normalising). Lean mirror — it's more defensible and the bait version is out of spec.

03 · findaletter.*
Matching portal Charity

"If you're a kid who's ever wondered if your dad cared." Kid types their name. System checks for a submitted letter. If a personalised one exists, they see it. If not, they submit a request so the parent sees it's been searched. Push through MoneySavingExpert-scale coverage.

04 · [founder].*
Personal seed Personal

The founder's own version. Personal to his kids, his voice, his style. Seeded with keywords across a timeline they'll eventually search. Links back to published books — the letters in book form. Whether they read them or not is theirs to decide.

🟢 Matching Mechanism

The match layer is what turns a publishing platform into a service. Low-friction on both sides.

↳ Parent submits a letter tagged to a child's identifiers (name, DOB-bracket, location cues, optional verifiers).
↳ Child searches their own name. If a letter exists for them specifically, it surfaces. If only a general CV exists, they see that.
↳ If nothing exists, the search itself becomes a ping — the parent gets notified "someone searched for you." That's the hook. Design open
↳ Verification + abuse handling is the real build problem. Needs proper thought before launch — impersonation and coercion are obvious attack vectors.
🟠 Audience — Who This Is For

This is not a product for the dad-rights-crusader crowd. They're not the buyer. They're posturing, and they'll hijack the framing.

↳ Target: quiet men. Low-grade jobs. Drinking through it. Going through the motions. Nearer the edge than they'd admit.
↳ Men outnumber women. The channel needs to be specific to them — not a neutral "parenting" platform that defaults to mums.
↳ Tone: not moralising. Not "be a better dad." Closer to "write it down before you can't."
↳ The marketing isn't guilt. It's permission — a structured artefact for people who don't have the words.
🟣 Model — Reese Witherspoon Reference

The business shape is borrowed. Use a platform to amplify other people's stories; publishing becomes the revenue; fame becomes infrastructure for others.

↳ Reese: book club → publishing imprint → more income than her film work. Used her reach to build a pipe others ship through.
↳ Second reference: the woman who bought her hometown cafeteria and employs a retiree for life each year. Didn't give money — gave a system. That's the philanthropy model.
↳ Applied here: Dad CVs is the pipe. The books (founder's letters) are the publishing imprint. Coverage from the charity surfaces feeds the pipe.
↳ Philanthropy test: it builds a system people can survive inside without you. If it collapses when the founder walks away, it's branding not philanthropy.
Separation note. Keep this ring-fenced from the gym-stand / supplement-kiosk idea. They share an AI-stack-builder mechanic but the audiences, tone, and legal exposure are completely different. Don't let the pill stand bleed into the fatherhood brand.
